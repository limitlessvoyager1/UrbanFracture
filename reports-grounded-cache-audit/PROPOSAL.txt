URBAN FRACTURE — GENERIC GROUNDED-POSE CACHE AUDIT / SCOPED PROPOSAL
UF-AUDIT-024 | 2026-11-12 | Audit only; repair requires approval

Conclusion
The generic grounded renderer has a confirmed shipping cache-validity defect.
Identical actor inputs at identical query time render differently depending on
earlier draws. Fresh-family fixture isolation hides this dependency; it does not
correct it. No mechanical combat defect is established by this audit. Production
source, existing tests, fixtures, QA gates, damage and timing remain unchanged.

Confirmed root cause — GPC-01 (priority 1)
dist/sprites.js:248 frameIndex selects an idle beat with floor(time*7)%2.
Line 327 also applies continuous idle squash: 1+sin(time*2.8)*.007.
Lines 376–379 declare a module-lifetime Map and cache grounded114 bitmaps using
an idle key of 'idle'+floor(time*4)%8. A hit draws the stored raster and returns.
The key aliases different native frames, and different squash within the same
frame index. Its two-second repetition permits old poses to survive across actors,
world/hero changes and time resets. The map's size limit is not validity control.
game.js drawActor sends eligible grounded actors through this production path.
Adding only native frameIndex to the key would leave continuous squash stale.

Fixture differences versus shipping defects
Earlier environment seed, tactic and family-isolation differences are harness
differences. Those were aligned before the remaining two discrepancies were
attributed. The controlled unmodified drawActor experiments establish a shipping
renderer defect independently of those fixtures. The instrumented original replay
then identifies precisely which earlier draw supplied the stale raster. The replay
resets fixture timelines between families; that magnifies history dependence and
does not establish campaign occurrence rate. The single .25-second-bucket case
also establishes the mechanism without such a reset. No damage, hitstun, fall,
grapple ownership or physical deadline failure follows from these pixel results.

Related conditional finding — GPC-02 (priority 2)
The native renderer reads hitFlash>0 (sprites.js:327), but the grounded cache key
does not. At the same .9 query time, warming with hitFlash=.2 and querying with 0
leaves 11,043 changed pixels, alpha IoU 1: stale brightness, unchanged silhouette.
This is reproduced through unmodified shipping drawActor with synthetic visual
flag states. Seven of eleven secondary types differ: thug, knifeThug, enforcer,
biker, sato, akari, bouncer. Flint, Broxton, Forest and Reina do not differ in the
tested state. Natural contact incidence and frequency are NOT established, and
this is not evidence of damage or hitstun failure. Treat it as a confirmed renderer
input omission with conditional gameplay reach, not seven proven encounter bugs.

Suspected only / separate scope
Other renderer inputs appear absent from the key, including render scale and
some action/alive-state detail. They have not received causal equivalent-input
tests here and are not confirmed defects or approved repair scope. Niko texture
seams, Broxton root projection, portrait404s and other combat issues stay separate.

Potential regressions and measured cost
More cache misses mean pixel readbacks, allocation and GC risk. Actor-only
headless CPU-wall probes (60 measured frames after 30 warm-up, identical thugs):
Actors     Existing cache mean     Cold every draw mean     Time-scoped model mean
1          .075 ms                 .658 ms                  .657 ms
4          .098 ms                 2.698 ms                 .837 ms
8          .128 ms                 5.450 ms                 .838 ms
Eight-actor time-scoped model p95=3.4 ms, max=4.0 ms; cold-each-draw p95=9.9 ms,
max=14.7 ms. This supports retaining same-time sharing. It is not a full-scene
budget, mixed-pose measurement, hardware/GPU benchmark or 60-FPS certification.
Unique poses may approach the cold cost. Nested alternate render clocks, paused
time, preview/playback, resets, sole placement and actual full-scene memory/frame
pacing require verification after approval. Stop and report unacceptable cost;
do not silently alter native animation timing to regain speed.
